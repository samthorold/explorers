# Instrument Runtimes

Measured wall-clock for the reference-mode runs and the other bins still in the workspace, to plan sweeps. Figures are from a laptop; treat them as orders of magnitude.

## Figures

**Archived bins.** The QD search and its research bins (`settling_time`, `energy_death_check`, `energy_bound_check`, `permanence_crosscheck`, `invasion_growth`, `role_emergence`, `radius_sweep`, `guild_census`, `role_diet_census`, `kin_killer_diet`, `reinvasion_barrier`, `fragility_audit` and the rest of `explorers-search`) left the workspace with the crate; their measured runtimes live in this file at the tag `atlas-search-archive`, alongside the code (`docs/agents/architecture.md`).

**`eval_scenarios`** (`explorers-genesis-eval`): ~3 min at a 500-tick horizon, release build.

- `reference_mode` (#764; `cargo run --release -p explorers-reference-modes --bin reference_mode`, in `explorers-search` until #782), the mode-1 mesocosm since #764 (9 × 9 nutrient cells, 243 founders, 1,377 standing carcasses at founding): 3,000 ticks with the defaults (seed 1, wear 0.2, a sample every 25 ticks) in 44 s, release build; seeds 2–5, run four at a time, all finished within a minute. The carcass pile, not the living roster, sets the cost: it grows to ~4,800 carcasses by tick 3,000 while the world holds a handful of agents. Since #785 a mobile agent's move finds the carcasses within its sensing range through a spatial grid instead of reading every carcass, bit-identically; that took seed 1 with the defaults, run alone, from 128 s to 116 s (−9 %). The scan was not the main cost: a `sample` profile after #785 puts over 90 % of the time in `World::step`'s own (inlined) body. The likely cost there is the energy ledger's per-tick residual pass, which calls `EnergyLedger::net_received` and `net_sent` (each a scan of every flow recorded this tick, one or more per carcass) for every pre-tick agent and carcass, so it grows with the square of the carcass pile (unconfirmed: the profile cannot separate inlined code). Paired (`--clear-at 1500`, 7,500 ticks in all): 121 s (seed 1). A run costs about eight times what it did before #764, so scale the #753 sweep figures below by that. Under #766's calibrated wear (the defaults since #766) the world holds ~200 producers instead of a handful, and a 3,000-tick run takes 122 s alone (seed 1) and ~3.5 min each with 8 sharing 8 cores: #766's 65-run grid (13 points × 5 seeds) took 25 min, 8 at a time on 8 cores (`docs/research/766-mode1-wear-recalibration.md`). Since #772 (decay 0.60 in the mesocosm, so decomposers persist), a paired run (`--clear-at 1500 --ticks 1500`, 4,500 ticks) takes 254–304 s each with 5 seeds sharing 8 cores (`docs/research/772-mesocosm-trophic-bound-reading.md`). Before #764 (#751), the mode-1 mesocosm (5 × 5 nutrient cells, 75 founders): 3,000 ticks with the defaults (seed 1, wear 0.2, a sample every 25 ticks) in 5.5 s, release build; 1,500 ticks in ~1.5 s. The settled world holds tens of agents, so a run is seconds and a wear sweep over many seeds is minutes. Measured (#753): 85 runs of 3,000 ticks (17 wear rates × 5 seeds) in 24 s wall-clock, 8 at a time through `xargs -P 8` on 8 cores; runs above the wear edge go extinct and take under 0.1 s. Paired (#752, `--clear-at 1500`, 3,000 ticks per arm, so 7,500 ticks in all): 18.7 s with decomposers, 15.8 s with `--no-decomposers` (seed 1, release build).

Memory is not the constraint (RSS < 100–600 MB); wall time is.

## Running long sweeps

- **Make a long sweep resumable.** Write results incrementally as JSON lines under `target/`, with a `--limit` to bound a call, so an interrupted sweep picks up where it stopped (the archived search bins shared `explorers_search::sweep` for this; restore it from the tag if a new sweep needs it). Keep artifacts under `target/`, not the scratchpad, which the OS tmp cleaner empties.
- **Drive long sweeps in-harness as one background process or a resumable loop.** The harness moves any foreground call past ~10 min into the background, and a sub-agent waiting on it may not be woken by the notification. So the orchestrating session drives the sweep and hands the write-up back, rather than a sub-agent "working while waiting".
- **Size a sweep by the terminal roster of its worst cells, never by a pilot's mean.** Worlds that persist with consumers settle to a handful of agents and are nearly free; gated worlds (monoculture, lockup) carry hundreds and dominate the tail. A 4-run pilot once missed a sweep's cost by two orders of magnitude.
- **A sweep that stops producing rows may be asleep, not hung.** On macOS, a laptop idle-sleeping on battery pauses the process, and `Instant` does not advance, so run budgets don't fire either. Check `ps -o cputime,etime` and `pmset -g log` before suspecting a hang. `caffeinate -i -w <pid>` keeps it awake — ask the owner before using it.
