# Instrument Runtimes

Measured wall-clock for the QD search and the research bins, to plan sweeps. Figures are from a laptop; treat them as orders of magnitude.

## Figures

**500-tick horizon (release build):** QD search ~20 min; `permanence_crosscheck`, `energy_bound_check`, `invasion_growth` ~20–30 min each; `eval_scenarios` ~3 min.

**Settled horizon T = 2000** (the default):

Dense LHS configs (listed in `docs/research/505*` and `508*`) cost 15–60 min each, because the evaluator's role snapshot is linear in agents × retained edges and one step can outrun the per-run guard, which fires only between steps.
- `role_emergence`, 35 cells × 8 seeds: ~3 h.
- `radius_sweep` (4 baselines × 8 levels × 5 seeds): ~1 h 45 min.
- `guild_census`: atlas ~1 min; 200 LHS configs ~60 min when split over disjoint `--configs` in parallel (one dense config blocks a whole process).
- `role_diet_census`: atlas ~40 s decoded, ~80 s at `founder_aggregation = 0` (the 95-cell atlas before #663); with `--fullness` on #663's 79-cell atlas, ~140 s decoded and ~125 s at fa 0. `kin_killer_diet` on #663's atlas: ~150 s decoded, ~140 s at fa 0 (`docs/research/656-fresh-atlas-verdict.md`). On the committed atlas (#677, 99 cells): `kin_killer_diet` 127 s decoded, 147 s at fa 0; `role_diet_census --fullness` 118 s / 133 s (`docs/research/670-cross-trait-verdict.md`). 200 LHS configs, one config at a time: ~2 h 50 min – 3 h 30 min with three sweeps sharing 8 cores. Which configs are heaviest shifts with the physics — worlds whose spared kin keep them alive and dense take longest. Split `--configs` over two processes so that `sample:20` does not hold the rest. The killing-grazer readout adds ~5 %.
- `reinvasion_barrier` on `sample:31`: ~7–25 s per mode (plain, `--accounting`, `--dispersal`), depending on the consumption settings; a 36-run (c, D) grid ~9 min.
- `fragility_audit` (#693): one cell is 1 + radii × draws rows × the seed ensemble, and the defaults (3 radii × 8 draws, 10 seeds) make it 250 rollouts, or ~25 000 on the 99-cell atlas. It rolls out as the search does: bloom stop 300:10, budgets 600 s + 600 s. Smoke (3 cells, 2 draws, T = 300): 210 rollouts in ~1 s, after a ~10 s release build. Measured: 1,842 s on the committed atlas (#693, 99 cells), but 8,218 s (2h17m) on #686's 83-cell atlas, whose worlds are denser. Budget 2–3 h. The bloom stop ends jittered worlds that bloom at tick 300, which cuts the dense tail, so a world must be dense without blooming 10× to reach the budgets. Drive it with `scripts/fragility-audit.sh`. It resumes per cell.
- `kin_killer_diet` with #700's leaching readout (section M), at `founder_aggregation = 0`: 3 cells × 5 seeds at T = 2000 in ~1 s per λ; the 99-cell atlas took 90 s at λ = 0 and 104–164 s per λ > 0, slower as λ rises (#700, measured). Rows are ~150 KB per cell (one entry per carcass). Drive it with `scripts/700-leaching.sh` (phase 1 the λ = 0 calibration, phase 2 a λ list). It resumes per λ and per cell.
- **QD search** (full box, 10 generations, default `--bloom-stop 300:10`): ~7.5–12 min before #652; ~13–19 min since, with `b` in the box (766 s for #656, 1119 s and 1142 s for #663's two seeds); 824 s for #670's seed 42 with `c_AH` in the box too, the committed atlas (#677), but 3858 s for its seed 43, whose dense generations ran 7 rollouts into the 600 s budget; 20 generations ~30 min. All of those ran 5 seeds per config; since #699 the default is 10, and on 32 identical bootstrap configs at T = 2000 10 seeds took 392 s against 280 s for 5 (×1.40) and 300 s with `--top-up-screen 5`, so the estimate was ~18–27 min (~14–20 min with top-up). Measured: #686's seed-42 standard search at 10 seeds took 3,285 s (55 min), because two dense generations took ~12 min each, with 4 rollouts into the 600 s budget. Budget an hour. A few dense generations dominate an unstopped search. Neither the search nor refinement has a per-rollout time limit, so one dense rollout can stall a refinement for hours.

Memory is not the constraint (RSS < 100–600 MB); wall time is.

## Running long sweeps

- **Every long bin is resumable.** Results are written incrementally as JSON lines under `target/` (shared `explorers_search::sweep`), with `--limit` to bound a call. Keep artifacts under `target/`, not the scratchpad, which the OS tmp cleaner empties.
- **Drive long sweeps in-harness as one background process or a resumable loop.** The harness moves any foreground call past ~10 min into the background, and a sub-agent waiting on it may not be woken by the notification. So the orchestrating session drives the sweep and hands the write-up back, rather than a sub-agent "working while waiting".
- **Size a sweep by the terminal roster of its worst cells, never by a pilot's mean.** Worlds that persist with consumers settle to a handful of agents and are nearly free; gated worlds (monoculture, lockup) carry hundreds and dominate the tail. A 4-run pilot once missed a sweep's cost by two orders of magnitude.
- **A sweep that stops producing rows may be asleep, not hung.** On macOS, a laptop idle-sleeping on battery pauses the process, and `Instant` does not advance, so run budgets don't fire either. Check `ps -o cputime,etime` and `pmset -g log` before suspecting a hang. `caffeinate -i -w <pid>` keeps it awake — ask the owner before using it.
