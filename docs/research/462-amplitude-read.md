# Issue #462 — the bloom amplitude as a reduced coordinate: does `m²` predict the bloom, and does the bloom predict the cliff?

**Status: measurement on existing data. Commits nothing in the stepper, evaluator
or search; adds no instrument.** [`462-radius-single-factor.md`](462-radius-single-factor.md)
§5 left #462 with a named next step: the radius is a real coordinate but *not* a
lockup coordinate — what it controls is **bloom amplitude**, and the cliff the
bloom meets is set by the rest of the config. That note asked two questions,
both answerable without new rollouts:

1. Does `m²` — alone, and against `solar_flux_magnitude` and
   `initial_population_size` — predict median peak population across configs?
2. Does peak population then predict the failure mode better than any raw
   dimension?

This note answers both on `target/permanence-crosscheck.jsonl` (#509: 282
configs × 8 seeds at `T = 2000`, SHA-1
`e92eb3709796251ea6e3c0dd6d506b777b16b731`), with the 32-level
`target/radius-sweep.jsonl` (SHA-1 `e6008b75a5771641811c893f5112c5cd3cfdfa75`)
as the single-factor corroboration. Stepper and search at `95032b0`.

## TL;DR

1. **Yes to the first question, once the amplitude is stated as a *factor*.**
   Peak population is bounded below by the founder count (`founders` is exactly
   `initial_population_size`, Spearman +1.00), so `N0` predicts raw peak
   definitionally. The bloom **factor** `peak / founders` is the coordinate-free
   amplitude, and **two numbers reach all thirty-two**: `log m²` + `log F` gives
   CV `R²` **0.37** against raw32's **0.37**. `N0` adds nothing to it (0.36).
   Adding the permanence groups goes *past* the raw box: `π4 + log m²` reads
   **0.42**.
2. **The amplitude law is a power law in the ceiling's geometric factor.** OLS
   over the LHS sample: `log₁₀(peak/founders) = 0.40·log₁₀ m² + 0.29·log₁₀ F −
   0.64`. The single-factor radius sweep, which moves `m²` alone at four fixed
   baselines, fits the same law within-baseline at slopes **0.31–0.76** (pooled
   0.55, per-baseline `R²` 0.48–0.94) — the cross-config exponent is not an
   artefact of the LHS draw.
3. **Yes to the second question, for the *measured* bloom.** Observed bloom
   factor reads CV `R²` **0.49** on live fraction and **0.41** on lockup, against
   the best single raw dimension (`light_competition_radius`) at **0.31 / 0.21**
   and all 32 raw coordinates at **0.40 / 0.35**. One measured number beats the
   whole box.
4. **But not for the *predicted* bloom, and that is the finding's limit.** The
   config-side amplitude `m², F` reads 0.28 / 0.19 — a tie with the bare radius,
   not a win. Splitting the observed bloom into what the config predicts and what
   the seed realises (`pred + resid`, two numbers) recovers 0.50 / 0.41: **about
   half of the amplitude's predictive power is realised, not configured.** The
   60 % the [π-space note](462-pi-space-explanatory-check.md) left unexplained is
   not all seed noise — a large share of it is a single mediating quantity the
   run itself reveals by ~its peak.
5. **The amplitude also shifts *which* cliff, which the radius note saw at four
   baselines and this sees across the box.** Binned by observed bloom factor,
   lockup rises 0.03 → 0.69 from no-bloom to 5–20×, then *falls* to 0.57 above
   20× as monoculture takes over (0.06 → 0.33 of seeds). Amplitude is not a
   lockup coordinate either; it is the coordinate that decides whether the world
   survives its own bloom at all.
6. **Consequence for #462: the reduced set is five or six numbers, not two.**
   `{log m², log F, r, mean_mobility, mean_kappa, contact_range_coefficient}`
   reads live **0.42** / lockup **0.34** / bloom **0.45** against raw32's
   0.40 / 0.35 / 0.37. The amplitude pair earns its place by explaining the
   *mechanism* and by beating the box on the bloom; it does not by itself
   replace the radius on outcome.

The LHS sample is the headline population — an unbiased draw of the search box.
Pooled figures (n = 279) are given where they differ; they do not change a
reading.

**Why linear.** The π-space note found quadratic and kNN fits *worse* than linear
at this n on every feature set; nothing here re-opens that. The power law is
fitted as a linear model in logs, which is the one non-linearity this data
supports, because it is the form the ceiling itself states.

## 2. The amplitude: what predicts the bloom

LHS sample, n = 198. `sd(log₁₀ bloom factor)` = 0.44, `sd(log₁₀ peak)` = 0.46.

| feature set | bloom factor | raw peak |
|---|---|---|
| `log m²` | 0.32 | 0.17 |
| `log m²` + `log F` | **0.37** | 0.18 |
| `log m²` + `log F` + `log N0` | 0.36 | 0.40 |
| `log F` + `log N0` | 0.05 | 0.12 |
| `r` (raw) | 0.23 | 0.15 |
| π4 (`ρ, I, Λ, κ_C`) | 0.06 | 0.05 |
| π4 + `log m²` | **0.42** | 0.25 |
| raw32 (unit) | 0.37 | 0.34 |

Read the two columns together. On **raw peak**, `N0` looks like the dominant
term (0.18 → 0.40 when added) — but `founders` *is* `initial_population_size`
and `peak ≥ founders` on every config in the file (minimum ratio exactly 1.000),
so that jump is arithmetic, not ecology. On the **bloom factor**, which divides
it out, `N0` is worth nothing (0.37 → 0.36) and the ceiling's geometric factor
carries the signal alone. This is the correction the radius note's phrasing
needed: the question "does `m²` against `F` and `N0` predict peak population"
has a misleading yes; the coordinate-free version has a clean one.

Two numbers (`log m²`, `log F`) equal all thirty-two, and five (π4 + `log m²`)
beat them. That is the first place in this line of work where the reduced
coordinates *outperform* the raw box rather than approaching it — and it happens
on the amplitude, not on the outcome.

**The fitted law.** Unstandardised OLS over the LHS sample:

```
log₁₀ (peak / founders)  =  0.40 · log₁₀ m²  +  0.29 · log₁₀ F  −  0.64
```

i.e. bloom factor ∝ `m^0.80 · F^0.29`. The ceiling `N̄_max = π_F·m²` bounds the
*sustained* count linearly in `m²`; the realised bloom rises as roughly its
square root, and the peak stays under the bare ceiling on 75 % of configs
(median `peak / m²` = 0.53). The flux enters weakly and positively, as a
ceiling-times-income reading predicts.

**Single-factor corroboration.** The radius sweep moves `r` alone at four fixed
baselines, so `m²` moves alone. Fitting the same law within each baseline:

| baseline | levels | slope on `log m²` | `R²` | bloom factor range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| recipe | 8 | +0.76 | 0.94 | 1.0× – 48× |
| `sample:110` | 8 | +0.31 | 0.67 | 1.0× – 7.3× |
| `sample:18` | 8 | +0.42 | 0.48 | 1.0× – 18× |
| `sample:188` | 8 | +0.75 | 0.82 | 1.0× – 74× |
| pooled | 32 | +0.55 | 0.60 | — |

Every baseline gives a positive exponent of the same order as the cross-config
0.40, and the two baselines with the widest amplitude range fit it tightest.
The spread (0.31–0.76) is the honest uncertainty on the exponent; a single
number is not yet warranted.

## 3. The cliff: what the bloom predicts

Same population, target = per-config seed fractions.

| outcome | sd | observed bloom | observed peak | predicted bloom (`m², F`) | realised residual | pred + resid | `m², F` | `r` | raw32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| live | 0.34 | **0.49** | 0.33 | 0.27 | 0.22 | **0.50** | 0.28 | 0.31 | 0.40 |
| lockup | 0.29 | **0.41** | 0.33 | 0.18 | 0.23 | 0.41 | 0.19 | 0.21 | 0.35 |
| extinction | 0.17 | 0.02 | 0.02 | 0.06 | −0.01 | 0.06 | 0.12 | 0.00 | 0.17 |
| consumers | 0.29 | 0.14 | 0.07 | −0.02 | 0.23 | 0.22 | 0.02 | −0.01 | 0.23 |

Three readings.

**The measured bloom beats the box.** One number — the bloom factor the ensemble
actually realised — carries more of live and lockup than all 32 searched
coordinates. The bloom factor also beats raw peak (0.49 vs 0.33 on live) for the
same reason as §2: dividing out the founder budget sharpens it.

**The predicted bloom does not.** `m², F` reads 0.28 / 0.19 against the bare
radius's 0.31 / 0.21 — a tie, inside the shuffle spread on lockup and slightly
behind on live. Amplitude is the right *mechanism*; it is not, on its own, a
better *config-side* predictor than the coordinate the π-space note already
named.

**The gap between them is structure, not noise.** Splitting the observed bloom
into the part the config predicts (`pred`) and the part the seeds realised on
top of it (`resid`, out-of-fold), the residual alone reads 0.22 / 0.23 — as much
as the whole config-side amplitude — and the two together reach 0.50 / 0.41.
So the ~60 % of outcome variance the π-space note could not explain from config
is not all seed noise: roughly half of what the amplitude explains is a realised
quantity, a single scalar the run exposes by its peak. That is a different kind
of claim from a coordinate reduction, and it has a use the coordinates do not
(§5). The `consumers` column is the sharpest case: config-side amplitude reads
**nothing** (−0.02) while the realised residual reads 0.23.

**The cliff moves with the amplitude.** Binned by observed bloom factor (LHS
sample; fractions are per-config means over finished seeds):

| bloom factor | n | live | lockup | extinction | consumers | median `m²` | median `r` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| = 1 (no bloom) | 103 | 0.85 | 0.03 | 0.10 | 0.42 | 49 | 13.5 |
| 1–2 | 52 | 0.75 | 0.13 | 0.06 | 0.39 | 90 | 11.0 |
| 2–5 | 20 | 0.44 | 0.49 | 0.03 | 0.21 | 121 | 7.5 |
| 5–20 | 12 | 0.22 | **0.69** | 0.01 | 0.13 | 600 | 4.2 |
| > 20 | 11 | 0.05 | 0.57 | 0.00 | 0.03 | 1369 | 2.6 |

Live falls monotonically, 0.85 → 0.05. Lockup does not: it peaks in the 5–20×
band and *falls* above it, because the tallest blooms die of something else
first. Per finished seed, the 5–20× band is 0.69 lockup / 0.06 monoculture; the
> 20× band is 0.57 lockup / **0.33 monoculture**. A bloom that overshoots far
enough collapses to a single dominant type before the death flux has silted the
nutrient. This is the box-wide version of the radius note's §3 finding that the
mode is baseline-dependent, and it closes the question that note left open in
the same direction: **amplitude is not a lockup coordinate either.** It is the
coordinate for *surviving your own bloom*; which way you fail if you do not is
decided elsewhere.

Half the sample (52 %) never blooms at all — bloom factor exactly 1.000. Split
in two, whether a config blooms is predicted at 0.24 (`log m²`) / 0.29
(`+ log F`) / 0.44 (raw32), and how tall it goes given that it blooms at 0.28 /
0.31 / 0.37. The raw box knows considerably more than the amplitude pair about
*whether* the bloom starts, and somewhat more about *how far* it goes.

## 4. What is left when amplitude is controlled for

Regressing the outcome residual (after the config-side amplitude) on the 32 raw
coordinates still reads CV `R²` 0.19 (live) and 0.16 (lockup); the largest ridge
weights name the same directions the π-space note's §3 did:

- live residual: `mean_mobility` −0.092, `world_extent` +0.072, `mean_kappa`
  +0.058, `light_competition_radius` +0.045
- lockup residual: `contact_range_coefficient` −0.073, `mean_mobility` +0.062,
  `mean_kappa` −0.046, `light_competition_radius` −0.044

The radius survives its own mediator — `m²` does not absorb all of `r`, which is
consistent with `r` also setting the contact geometry, not only the count
ceiling. Candidate reduced sets, config-side only, 5-shuffle means:

| coordinate set | dims | live | lockup | bloom |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `log m², log F` | 2 | 0.28 | 0.19 | 0.37 |
| + `r` | 3 | 0.36 | 0.23 | 0.37 |
| + `mean_mobility, mean_kappa` | 4 | 0.35 | 0.24 | 0.41 |
| + `r, mean_mobility, mean_kappa` | 5 | 0.43 | 0.28 | 0.41 |
| + `r, mean_mobility, mean_kappa, contact_range_coefficient` | 6 | **0.42** | **0.34** | **0.45** |
| six raw dims (π-space note §3) | 6 | 0.41 | 0.37 | 0.41 |
| raw32 | 32 | 0.40 | 0.35 | 0.37 |

Six numbers reproduce the box on outcome and beat it on the bloom. The
amplitude pair's contribution over the previous note's six raw dims is the bloom
column (0.45 vs 0.41) and the mechanism; on live and lockup the two six-sets are
within the shuffle spread of each other. **The effective dimension of the search
box is ~6, confirmed twice by different routes** — which is the claim #462 needs,
and it is now a measured one.

## 5. What this settles for #462

- **The reduced coordinate set is `{amplitude, radius, mobility, kappa, contact
  range}` — about six numbers, and the amplitude is `m^0.8 F^0.29`, not `m²`
  raw.** That is enough to justify either a lower-dimensional `decode` or a
  range-shrink of the other 26 dims; §3 of the acceptance criteria can be met
  without new rollouts, on an independent draw (below).
- **The permanence groups are not useless — they are the wrong *level*.** π4 +
  `log m²` beats raw32 on the amplitude (0.42 vs 0.37) while reading ~0.05 on
  outcome. The groups describe the community's dynamics, the ceiling describes
  its budget, and the outcome needs both plus the cliff directions.
- **A realised observable is worth more than any coordinate.** The bloom factor
  measured on a run reads 0.49 / 0.41 against the best config-side 0.31 / 0.21.
  For the *search*, that suggests a cheaper use than re-parameterising: the bloom
  factor is available long before `T` and could serve as an early-stop or
  fitness signal. That is a separate proposal, not this note's finding, and it
  needs the peak's *tick* — which `permanence_crosscheck` does not record.

## 6. What this does not settle

- **No independent draw.** Every fit here is cross-validated within the seed-421
  LHS sample, the same data the π-space note used and the same data the six raw
  dims were *selected* on. The six-dim sets in §4 are therefore optimistic by an
  unmeasured amount, and #462's acceptance ("CV explanatory power against raw 32
  on an independent draw") is **not** met by this note. A fresh LHS draw at a
  different seed, scored at `T = 2000`, is what closes it.
- **The atlas is a 500-tick object** (#494). Both populations here are scored at
  `T = 2000`, but the 81 atlas configs were *selected* at 500; the pooled column
  inherits that. Nothing in §2–§4 rests on the atlas rows.
- **Interactions, still.** The power law is the only non-linearity fitted. Whether
  the radius's effect differs at high and low flux — the radius note's §6
  question — needs the two-factor sweep, not this data.
- **The peak's timing.** The mediation reading in §3 would be far stronger with
  the tick at which the peak occurred (does the bloom predict the cliff *before*
  the cliff?). No sweep on disk records it; adding `peak_tick` to the sweep row
  shape is a one-line change to whichever instrument runs next.
- **The 52 % that never bloom.** Treating a point mass at factor 1.0 with a
  linear model in logs is crude; a two-stage read (§3) is reported but not
  modelled properly. The raw box's advantage there (0.44 vs 0.29) is the largest
  unexplained gap in this note.
- **Guilds** are untouched, as before: 16 positive seeds in 2186 (#519).
