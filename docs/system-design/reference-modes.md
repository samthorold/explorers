# Reference Modes

The purpose of the world, stated as behaviour over time ([system design](README.md), *Purpose before structure*). Each reference mode is a characteristic dynamic of real ecosystems, drawn from the [ecology layer](../ecology/), that the world must be able to produce from its own rules. Each one is observed at the patch scale, one to three nutrient cells, over a horizon stated in the world's own time constants.

The ecology is encoded well when the world reproduces these behaviours. Encoding each mechanism faithfully is necessary but not sufficient: a world can be faithful mechanism by mechanism and still produce none of them.

## How a reference mode is read

A reference mode is read by a **perturbation experiment on a known state**, the way ecology establishes these dynamics: by gap creation, removal and exclusion experiments, often in mesocosms built to isolate one case.

1. Take a **known state**: a parameter set and an initial distribution, run under the rules until it settles, then snapshotted.
2. Apply the mode's canonical perturbation at the patch scale. Run the same state unperturbed beside it as the control.
3. Read the response over the mode's horizon. The mode is produced when the perturbed patch shows the stated behaviour and the control does not show it in its place.

A known state is either designed or found:
- **Designed mesocosms come first.** These are small worlds of a few patches, set up to hold the case a mode needs. For gap dynamics, that is a closed canopy with resident decomposers. They isolate the mode, run in minutes, and need no search.
- **Found states come second.** These are settled snapshots of worlds that [genesis](genesis-search.md) finds sensible. They test generality: whether the mode also arises in worlds nobody designed.

The one qualification for a known state, designed or found, is that **its control persists at the patch scale over the mode's horizon**. If the unperturbed patch is itself collapsing or blooming, the response cannot be told apart from the drift.

Why this form:
- **The response is emergent even when the state is designed.** The by-construction concern is about the outcome: a scenario arranged so that the outcome must happen tests wiring, not emergence ([expected properties](expected-properties.md), *By-construction examples test wiring, not emergence*). A designed state authors the starting point and the trigger. What follows the perturbation comes from the rules alone.
- **The perturbation supplies the rare event.** Waiting for a mode to occur on its own, say for a large producer to die in the patch being watched, turns the reading into a statistical search over many runs. The perturbation makes the event happen where and when the reading looks.
- **The paired control isolates the response.** The same state is run with and without the perturbation, so the difference is the response to the perturbation, not to anything else in the run.
- **It reads at the mode's own scale.** A mesocosm over a few generations is cheap, so a mode can be read every time the physics changes.

Reaching sensible worlds and perturbing a known state are separate concerns. Genesis answers where in parameter space sensible worlds live, at the world scale. Reference modes ask how a state responds, at the patch scale. They meet only where found states test generality.

## The first set

These four are in the first set because between them they exercise every flow and both currencies. Light competition, nutrient uptake and sharing, consumption of living targets, decomposition, leaching, growth, reproduction and dispersal each carry at least one of them. They also do not depend on any coupling mechanism beyond the committed physics.

The modes are worked in the order 1, 2, 4, 3:
- **Colonisation overshoot** needs no consumers, only producers and a working decomposer guild, so its mesocosm is the easiest to build. It is the first case for the whole method: designed state, persisting control, perturbation and paired reading. The guild it needs is read first, in the same mesocosm ([end state](end-state.md#the-decomposer-guild)).
- **Gap dynamics** adds the detrital loop and light competition.
- **Divergent recovery** reuses gap dynamics' detrital loop with that loop cut.
- **Shifting patch mosaic** is unforced, needs several patches cycling, and depends on consumers moving, so its mesocosm is the hardest.

### 1. Colonisation overshoot

**Behaviour.** An empty patch is colonised. Producer biomass rises, peaks, and declines to a lower level, around which it then fluctuates as the patch's producers come to have mixed ages.

**Perturbation.** Clear every agent and carcass from a patch of a known state. The patch's pool keeps its nutrient, and its neighbours are untouched, so colonisation comes from dispersal. The control is the same patch left alone, with producers of mixed ages.

**Horizon.** About one lifespan of the colonising cohort.

**Mesocosm.**
- **Parameters.** The mesocosm owns its parameters, its founders' traits and their reserve. They are stated in full, with nothing derived from another world at run time. A designed state is authored, so its founders are named, not taken from some world's mean traits.
  - **Where they come from.** The world parameters descend from the recipe of the last atlas (atlas:31), rescaled to the mesocosm's area, with somatic wear on. The founders are that recipe's mean traits with all trophic investment at one vertex: autotrophy for a producer, heterotrophy for a decomposer.
  - **Trophic transfer.** Flow 7 is the one place where the recipe was out of bound. Its decay of 2.38 lies outside flow 7's domain bound, so the mesocosm sets `trophic_distance_decay` to 0.60. That is the bound's midpoint, a factor of about 0.4 at the vertex distance. The mesocosm keeps the recipe's `base_trophic_efficiency` of 0.78, which is in bound (world rules, flow 7, *What the two parameters stand for, and their domain bounds*).
  - **Feeding reach.** The mesocosm's decomposers reach litter by growth, through the body-extent term of feeding reach (world rules, flow 3), not by travel alone. In the domain, litter decomposers are saprotrophs that grow into litter, and cord-formers span many pieces of it ([fungi](../ecology/fungi.md), *How a saprotroph reaches its litter*). `√structure` is the domain's dense-mycelium end of `M^(1/D)`, so the term's form stands. The domain gives the magnitude only as an order, many pieces of litter rather than one, and no figure that carries over to this world's units. So the coefficient is set by the founders' catchments: reach at the value where the decomposer founders' catchments tile the stand (*Decomposer founders*, below).
    - **The domain's magnitude is a stand's litter ratio, and reach follows from it.** Fungal biomass is about 0.07–0.6 % of the litter it lives in: a **litter ratio** of about **170 to 1,400** ([fungi](../ecology/fungi.md), *How much litter one saprotroph commands*). That is the whole stand's decomposers against its litter, measured in bulk, not one individual's catchment. A founder's catchment equals the stand's litter ratio only when the founders' catchments tile the stand without overlapping. Where they overlap, its neighbours share every carcass in reach, and what each founder lives on is still the litter ratio ([826-mode1-catchment.md](../research/826-mode1-catchment.md)). So the litter ratio sets the decomposer founders' count (*Decomposer founders*, below), and reach is what lets catchments tile at that count: about `√(A / (π n))` for `n` founders on area `A`. The bullets below read the ratio as one founder's catchment, the case where catchments tile.
      - **Which pieces count.** The ratio counts the founding stand's litter: 3.18 structure a carcass, 17 to a cell. Against a founder's 2.46, it is `R ≈ 1.29 k`.
      - **Why this ratio and not a distance.** No genet span carries over to this world's units. The ratio does, and it has the form the body-extent term already has: reach grows as `√S`, so the litter in reach grows in proportion to the body.
      - **It agrees with the founder's energy budget.** At `R ≈ 170` (k ≈ 130), a founder paying 0.20 a tick at efficiency 0.31 breaks even on the founding stand's litter, at about 0.0048 a carcass from about 135 carcasses. So the domain's lower end is where the specialist begins to pay its way.
      - **What makes the advantage a specialist's.** Reach is in proportion to effective heterotrophy, so the area it covers goes as its square. A founder at the vertex (`h` 1.07) commands about fifty times the litter of a mixotroph at 0.15. The specialist's advantage on litter is that catchment, not efficiency (world rules, flow 7, *Why mode 1's failure does not bring it out*).
      - **The domain's upper end fills the mesocosm.** At `R ≈ 1,400` (k ≈ 1,090), a founder reaches about 45 on a torus about 90 across: the hectare-scale genet in a world of nine by nine cells.
      - **Why a lower upkeep is not the lever.** Dormancy cuts a decomposer's upkeep per unit of standing biomass, and mode 1's founders meet the condition that holds it in reserve: they starve after drawing their earmark down (world rules, flow 8; [822-mode1-lean-time-reserve.md](../research/822-mode1-lean-time-reserve.md)). But this world's drain capability belongs to the individual, not to its mass, and each drainer applies it in full to every carcass in reach. An inactive fraction that sheds cost and capability together leaves each active unit's budget where it was, apart from a factor of up to 2 from saturation. Mode 1's active tissue earns 1–14 % of its upkeep ([814-mode1-combined-reading.md](../research/814-mode1-combined-reading.md)). So dormancy would lengthen the dying, not found a guild, and it stays in reserve until active tissue pays its way.
      - Per unit of active tissue, mode 1's founder upkeep is in the domain's range. Over a litter half-life it spends 9–18 times its body (at L 0.5–1), against the domain's 2–29. Its catchment is what is short.
    - **Why living tissue must resist.** Feeding reach is one predicate for living and dead targets. A living producer is spared only by recognition, and it is as easy to assimilate as its carcass, so a reach that spans many pieces of litter also spans the producers standing among them. In the domain, reach does not turn a saprotroph into a pathogen; the defence of living tissue does ([fungi](../ecology/fungi.md), *Reach does not decide diet; defence does*). Without that defence, reach turns the decomposer founders into grazers ([796-body-reach-sweep.md](../research/796-body-reach-sweep.md)). So the mesocosm's living tissue resists its consumers at `ρ = 1` (world rules, *Living tissue resists drains*), and a guild that persists by grazing the stand is not a saprotroph guild.
    - **Why the coefficient is not picked.** Any one count, and above all the diagnosis's break-even of about 1.5 carcasses in reach ([764-founder-death-diagnosis.md](../research/764-founder-death-diagnosis.md)), would choose the parameter to flatter the scenario, which `viability.md` rules out for a world default. The domain's own ratio sets it instead.
  - **Drain kinetics, and what the litter can feed.** Carcass decomposition saturates in its decomposers, with a decay constant anchored to producer lifespan (world rules, *Carcass decomposition saturates in its decomposers*). Under it, the litter sets a ceiling on decomposers. The standing stock relaxes until decay matches the litter that deaths add, so what a settled stand sustains is that inflow, not its standing stock: 0.42 energy a cell-tick, 97 % from producer deaths, or 0.13 assimilated at flow 7's 0.31 ([804-mode1-litter-supply.md](../research/804-mode1-litter-supply.md)). A decomposer founder's upkeep under a per-agent base rate is about 0.41, so one cell's inflow would pay for about a third of a founder. A guild must therefore collect from many carcasses, the domain's cord-former again. In this world that needs body reach, which also exposes living producers (*Why living tissue must resist*).
    - **Upkeep.** The base rate scales linearly with structure, at a mass-specific rate that conserves the median established producer's base-plus-structure cost (world rules, flow 8), with that producer's structure read on the mesocosm's own control ([804-mode1-litter-supply.md](../research/804-mode1-litter-supply.md), [814-mode1-combined-reading.md](../research/814-mode1-combined-reading.md)). A small decomposer founder then pays a small fraction of the per-agent base rate. Trait maintenance and movement cost are unchanged.
    - **Both constraints the arithmetic names are in the setup.** Upkeep scales with size (world rules, flow 8), under which one cell's inflow pays for about one founder. Living tissue resists drains, at `ρ = 1` for mode 1's consumers (world rules, *Living tissue resists drains*), so body reach does not expose the stand.
  - **Why they are not re-read from the latest atlas.** An atlas is searched for a fixed physics. Each change to the physics makes it stale, and a new search costs hours. The mesocosm, by contrast, is cheap to recalibrate. When the physics changes, its parameters are adjusted against its own control: the control must persist (below), and any adjusted value must stay inside its domain bound. Whether a mode carries over to worlds nobody designed is what found states test. That is a separate reading, not something the mesocosm should inherit through its parameters.
- **Wear rate.** Producer lifespans of a few hundred ticks, shorter than the leaching half-life. This keeps the cohort's turnover apart from the slow return of nutrient through carcasses.
- **Size.** A toroidal world of 9 × 9 nutrient cells. The perturbed patch is the 3 × 3 block of cells at the centre, three cells across, the largest a patch can be. A single cell holds too few producers for its control to persist. The block has a full ring of cells around it as a source of colonists, and the wrap is far enough away that colonists do not arrive back from the far side at once. The clearance and every reading of the patch take the whole block.
- **Standing carcasses.** A colonised stand has litter, so every cell starts with standing carcasses, which carry the decomposer founders until producer deaths take over. Each cell gets a settled recipe world's carcass stock per cell area, as producer litter: 17 carcasses holding 54 energy and 58 nutrient in all. Their nutrient comes out of the cell's pool after the founders have bound theirs, as the founders' does (world rules, *Founders bind nutrient at world creation*), so total nutrient at creation is the pool.
- **Founding clusters.** Producers and decomposer founders are two unrelated founding clusters (world rules, flow 4, *Reproductive isolation follows ancestry*), because in the domain decomposers and plants do not cross ([trophic roles](../ecology/trophic-roles.md), *A decomposer lineage stays a decomposer*). Isolated by trait distance instead, the decomposer founders' line leaves the heterotroph vertex by crossing with producers ([833-mode1-recruitment-diagnostic.md](../research/833-mode1-recruitment-diagnostic.md), [837-mode1-crossing.md](../research/837-mode1-crossing.md)). The compatibility scale is the world's, `s` = 100. The mesocosm is insensitive to `s` once it is well above the spread drift builds within a cluster over the run: pairwise `d²` grows by about 16 a generation, so even a generation every 12 ticks gives `d` about 63 in 3,000 ticks, which at `s` = 100 is `c` ≥ 0.82 within a cluster, while between clusters `c` is about `e⁻⁵⁰` at any `s`.
- **Decomposer founders.** Their number is set by the founding stand's **litter ratio**: the stand's litter against the founders' combined structure. Each founder keeps the recipe's mean traits at the heterotroph vertex, and the litter keeps its settled recipe stock. The founders are scattered at random, as every founder in the mesocosm is, and their reach is set so their catchments tile the stand on average.
  - **Why at random and not on a lattice.** The domain's founders arrive scattered with their leaves, and a lattice would author the tiling the reading is meant to test. Scattered founders overlap where they fall close and leave litter out of reach where they fall far apart, so at the tiling reach a founder earns somewhat less than a lone founder would: about a fifth less at ten founders.
  - **Why the count and not the litter or the founders.** The litter stock and the founders' traits are both taken from the recipe, with a measured source. The count is not; one decomposer a cell was never derived. It is the free number, and the domain constrains it.
  - **Why the domain constrains it.** A stand's founding decomposers are a small part of its litter. Fungi are about 0.6 % of fresh oak litter's mass and grow into it, peaking near 5 % and settling near 2.6 %; Baldrian's 0.07–0.6 % is lower still ([fungi](../ecology/fungi.md), *Decomposer biomass against its supply*). So the founding litter ratio is about 170 or more, and the ratio falls as the guild grows. One founder a cell is a litter ratio of 22, about the domain's peak, at founding.
  - **Why reach cannot stand in for it.** Carcass decomposition saturates in its decomposers (world rules, flow 7), so a stand yields at most `k_d · S` a carcass, however many founders reach it. With 81 founders and body reach across 170–1,400 times a founder's structure, each carcass is in reach of 8 to 64 founders, and they drain the stand at 87–98 % of its ceiling. That ceiling, shared 81 ways, is about a quarter of a founder's upkeep, so every founder dies early at every reach ([826-mode1-catchment.md](../research/826-mode1-catchment.md)).
  - **The stepper's budget agrees.** A founder alone on its carcasses breaks even on about 135 of them. The founding stand holds 1,377, so it carries about 10 lone founders, a litter ratio of about 175. That is the domain's founding end.
  - **The guild comes from recruitment.** The *heterotroph guild* is read from tick 2,250 and needs at least five members on every sample and a birth. A handful of founders cannot hold it alone. Founders that break even reproduce into it, as the domain's founders grow into their litter, and that is the reading the guild already asks for: a line that keeps recruiting may lose every founder.
- **Community.** Producers and decomposers, with no consumers. Producers carry the cohort and storage loops. Decomposers carry the return from carcasses. Mode 1 reads the arm with decomposers only, because the domain observes the overshoot in stands whose litter decomposers return, never without them ([end state](end-state.md#mode-1-colonisation-overshoot)). The mesocosm's arm without decomposers is divergent recovery's contrast, not mode 1's, and the arm with them is also the decomposer guild's setup, which mode 1 waits on.
  - **What must persist is a heterotroph-dominant decomposer guild,** in the arm with decomposers. That is a *heterotroph guild* of the decomposer role whose members invest mainly in heterotrophy, not autotrophy, with sustained size and recruitment. In the domain, litter is returned by heterotrophs that live on it: fungi, bacteria and detritivores. Plants do not feed on litter; depolymerising dead matter is microbial work ([trophic roles](../ecology/trophic-roles.md), *Producers do not mineralise detritus themselves*).
  - **Why neither weaker reading suffices.** The founders surviving by id is not a domain property. Under the guild reading, a line that keeps recruiting can lose every founder, and that is a pass. Any agent with the decomposer role is too weak. The role is read from income, so producer-line mixotrophs living on carcasses carry it, and in this mesocosm they take 90–99 % of the carcass drain ([781-movement-reading.md](../research/781-movement-reading.md)). A loop closed by producers eating their own litter is not the detrital loop the domain describes, so it is a discrepancy to explain, not a pass.
- **If the control does not persist.** This can happen when wear comes on or when the physics changes. Then the mesocosm's parameters are adjusted, wear rate first, and each adjusted value must stay within its domain bound. Wear is not turned off.

**Why it is here.** At the scale of a stand, biomass after colonisation usually peaks and declines rather than levelling off smoothly. That is the aggradation and transition phases of forest development, before the shifting-mosaic steady state ([disturbance and succession](../ecology/disturbance-and-succession.md), *Biomass after colonisation: smooth accumulation or peak and decline*). The mode is the first step of a sequence the next modes continue. The declining cohort breaks up into gaps (gap dynamics), and patches cycling out of phase make the mosaic (shifting patch mosaic).

**Dynamic hypothesis.** Three loops, each with its own job. The ecology names all three, and which dominates depends on the system. In this world the first is primary. The loops are the design's explanation of how the world produces the mode, not hypotheses of the end state: the ecology does not rank the three mechanisms, so no reading rule follows from it for any one loop. Their predictions say what to look at when the mode's reading surprises us ([end state](end-state.md#mode-1-colonisation-overshoot)).

- **Peak and decline come from cohort synchrony.** Colonists that establish within a short window form an even-aged cohort. An amplifying loop of producer biomass, reproduction and dispersal fills the patch. The shading loop then closes it to later recruits: bigger producers take a larger share of light, grow, and shade newcomers further (world rules, flow 1). So the patch holds one cohort, which wears out together through somatic wear and dies over a short span. The delay in this damping loop is the cohort's lifespan. That is why the patch overshoots rather than levelling off.
  *Predictions:* the decline begins about one cohort lifespan after colonisation, not when the pool runs dry. Colonisation spread over a longer window, so that the cohort is uneven in age, gives a lower peak.
- **The timing of the peak comes from internal storage.** An agent's free nutrient store and its reproductive nutrient earmark act like a cell quota. Growth and reproduction draw on what the agent already holds, so they continue for a while after the patch's pool is exhausted. This is a short delay, and a minor one.
  *Prediction:* births continue for a while after the patch's available pool reaches zero.
- **The settled level comes from nutrient held in the dead.** Colonising producers bind the pool into living bodies, so near the peak almost all of the patch's nutrient is alive. Once the cohort turns over, a standing share of it is always in transit through carcasses until it is drained or leaches, and the pool does not diffuse between cells to make it up. The settled living biomass therefore sits below the peak by that share.
  *Prediction:* the slower carcasses return nutrient, the lower the settled level relative to the peak. The patch's settled level is lowest with no decomposers and a low leaching rate.

### 2. Gap dynamics

**Behaviour.** A large producer dies, leaving a light gap and a carcass. Decomposers converge on the carcass and return its nutrient to the pool. Fast-growing producers colonise the gap, and slower, larger producers overtop them and close it.

**Perturbation.** Kill the largest producer in a patch of a known state.

**Horizon.** A few consumer lifespans, and shorter than the leaching half-life. The pulse back to the pool comes through decomposers, not through leaching.

**Why it is here.** Gap dynamics are the spatial unit of succession: a local reset inside an otherwise stable matrix ([disturbance and succession](../ecology/disturbance-and-succession.md), *Gap dynamics*, *Priority effects*). In this world one death touches light competition (the shading that held the gap closed), the carcass pool, decomposition, the nutrient pulse and the race between producer strategies. The mode is a causal chain through almost every flow, so it fails visibly at the first broken link.

### 3. Shifting patch mosaic

**Behaviour.** Consumers deplete a patch of producers and move on, and the patch recovers. Neighbouring patches go through the same cycle out of phase. Every patch rises and falls while the region as a whole persists.

**Perturbation.** None. The mode is an unforced behaviour, read by comparing neighbouring patches over the same span of a run from a known state. The control is the reading itself: the patches' cycles must be out of phase, not merely each cycling.

**Horizon.** Several consumer lifespans per cycle, with several cycles observed.

**Why it is here.** Regional persistence through local turnover is how space stabilises interacting species. A local population can go extinct while recolonisation from neighbours keeps the region occupied ([spatial ecology](../ecology/spatial-ecology.md), *Patch dynamics and metapopulations*; [stability and resilience](../ecology/stability-and-resilience.md), *Space stabilises through asynchrony*). It tests consumption of living targets, consumer movement and dispersal, and whether asynchrony arises without any external forcing, which this world has none of.

### 4. Divergent recovery

**Behaviour.** Two patches lose their producers alike. In the patch where decomposers remain, nutrient returns to the pool and producers recover. In the patch where decomposers are lost, the pool stays depleted and recovery takes many producer generations, driven only by leaching.

**Perturbation.** Remove the producers from two comparable patches of a known state, and remove the decomposers from one of them as well. Its carcasses stay where they are.

**Horizon.** The recovering patch recovers within a few consumer lifespans. The depleted patch stays depleted for longer than the leaching half-life.

**Why it is here.** Detritus stabilises a food web only while something eats it. When decomposers are scarce or out of reach, the dead pool integrates mortality with no damping ([stability and resilience](../ecology/stability-and-resilience.md), *Donor control holds only while decomposers track the pile*). The mode shows the detrital loop by its absence as well as its presence. It is the patch-scale version of what [expected properties](expected-properties.md) calls nutrient lockup. Whether the depleted state is a true alternative stable state, held by a reinforcing loop with hysteresis, or only slow recovery, is for the dynamic hypothesis to say. The domain has both ([disturbance and succession](../ecology/disturbance-and-succession.md), *Alternative stable states*). With leaching, this world's lockup is partial and slow, not absorbing ([world rules](world-rules.md), *Carcass energy decays only through agents; carcass nutrient leaches*).

## Next: mutualism

**Behaviour.** Coupled producer–decomposer pairs outperform uncoupled neighbours in nutrient-poor patches ([fungi](../ecology/fungi.md), *Mycorrhizal strategy*).

It follows the first set, not alongside it, because it is a coupling layered on the detrital loop that modes 2 and 4 exercise. A partnership needs decomposers that track carcasses and partners at patch scale. Until the world produces gap dynamics and divergent recovery, a failure of mutualism cannot be told apart from a failure of the loop beneath it.
